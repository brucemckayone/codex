## Getting paid on Revelations

*Effective [date]. These terms are between you and **[Legal entity]**
([Company number], [Registered address]).*

### 1. How it works

When someone buys your work, **we** sell it to them: we take the payment and
are responsible for it. We then pay you, and the organisation you sell through,
your shares of each sale. All amounts are in pounds sterling (£) and are worked
out in whole pence.

### 2. Setting up payouts

- You are paid through **Stripe**. To receive money you set up a Stripe account
  through Revelations. Stripe checks your identity and bank details.
  **[Lawyer: Stripe's required Connected Account Agreement wording — note P1.]**
- You have one payout account, whichever organisations you belong to.
- You can update the bank account your money is paid into at any time, from
  your payout dashboard in Studio. Only you can open your dashboard.
- Before you can sell priced content, your payouts must be set up. An
  organisation's owner must set up payouts before the organisation can offer
  subscriptions.
- If you make sales before your payouts are set up, or Stripe pauses your account
  (for example to re-check your identity), your money is **kept for you** and
  paid as soon as your account is ready. It is never forfeited. While money is
  waiting for you, we email you a reminder once a month.

### 3. Fees

| Fee | Default |
|---|---|
| Platform fee | **10%** of each sale |
| Organisation fee on one-off sales | **10%** of what is left after the platform fee |
| Organisation fee on subscriptions | **15%** of what is left after the platform fee |

- The platform fee is never less than **25p**, because the card processing fee
  we pay can be more than 10% of a cheap sale. When 10% would be less than 25p,
  the difference is taken from the creator's share first, then from the
  organisation's.
- We may agree different fees with an organisation or with you. Fees are worked
  out at the time of each sale.
- **If we raise a fee, we email you at least 30 days before it applies**, and it
  applies only to sales made after that. If we lower a fee, it applies straight
  away.
- We pay the card processing fees and the banks' dispute fees.

**Examples, one-off sales with no revenue-share agreement:**

| Sale | Platform | Organisation | You |
|---|---|---|---|
| £10.00 | £1.00 | 90p | £8.10 |
| £1.00 | 25p (the minimum) | 9p | 66p |

### 4. Revenue-share agreements

An organisation and a creator can agree a different split. This is called a
**revenue-share agreement**.

- An agreement is between you and the organisation, agreed with its owner. The
  owner proposes a share; you can accept it, decline it or counter it, and the
  owner can do the same with your counter. It starts when one side accepts the
  other's proposal.
- A share is a percentage of **what is left after the platform fee**. A 70%
  share of a £10.00 sale is 70% of £9.00 = £6.30.
- There are two kinds of agreement:
  - **One-off sales.** It covers sales of your own content and courses in that
    organisation.
  - **Subscriptions.** Money from an organisation's subscriptions is **not**
    divided by who made which content. Each payment goes into one pool for the
    organisation, and the pool is divided between the creators who have
    subscription agreements, by their shares. The organisation keeps the rest.
- **If an organisation has no subscription agreements, all of its subscription
  money (after the platform fee) goes to the organisation.** You earn from an
  organisation's subscriptions only through an agreement.
- **Course subscriptions are different.** A subscription to one of your courses
  pays you as a sale does: after the platform fee, the organisation takes its
  subscription fee (15%, or your agreed share instead) and you get the rest.
- An agreement has a **review date**. It is a reminder, not an end date. We email
  both sides 30 days before it. After it passes, the agreement **carries on**
  until one side ends it.
- **Either side can end an agreement at any time.** It ends straight away. You
  are paid for every sale made while it was in place, and for none made after.
  There is no part-payment for part of a period.
- If you leave the organisation or are removed from it, your agreements there
  end at that moment. Money you earned before then is still paid to you.

**Example: a £10.00 subscription payment.** Platform £1.00, leaving £9.00.

| Agreements in the organisation | Each share |
|---|---|
| None | Organisation £9.00 |
| You at 30% | You £2.70 · Organisation £6.30 |
| You at 30%, another creator at 60% | You £2.70 · Other creator £5.40 · Organisation 90p |

Every share is in whole pence. On one-off sales the platform and organisation
fees are rounded **up** to the penny and you get the rest. On an organisation's
subscriptions each share is rounded **down**, and any leftover penny goes to the
organisation.

### 5. When you are paid

- Your share is moved to your Stripe balance when the sale is made.
- From there it is paid to your bank **after 14 days**. That is the window in
  which a buyer can be refunded. We set the payout schedule for every account,
  and it cannot be changed.

### 6. Refunds, chargebacks and fraud

- Refunds are decided by Revelations support, within 14 days of a sale. Creators
  and organisations cannot issue refunds.
- **When a sale is refunded, your share is reduced by the same proportion.** If
  half of a sale is refunded, half of your share of it is taken back.
- **Chargebacks.** If a buyer's bank reverses a payment, your share is taken back
  in proportion while the bank decides. If we win, it is paid back to you. If we
  lose, it stays taken back. We pay the bank's dispute fee.
- If a buyer's card issuer warns us that a payment looks fraudulent, we refund it
  in full and your share is taken back.
- **If your Stripe balance cannot cover an amount taken back**, it is recorded as
  an amount you owe. It is deducted from your future earnings until it is
  cleared. We tell you when this happens, and it shows in your earnings.
- If you owe **£50 or less** in total and earn nothing for 12 months, we write
  off what is left.
- If you owe **more than £50** in total and it has not been recovered from your
  earnings within 90 days, we will send you an invoice for the whole amount
  outstanding.

### 7. Organisations and their owner

- Every organisation has **one owner**: the person who created it, until they
  hand it over.
- The owner receives the organisation's share of every sale.
- Only the owner can hand the organisation to someone else. They offer it to an
  admin or creator of the organisation, who must accept within 7 days. The owner
  must hand it over before they can leave.
- From the moment the new owner accepts, the organisation's share goes to them.
  Money earned before then stays with the previous owner, who becomes an admin.
  If the new owner's payouts are not set up, the organisation's money is kept
  for them until they are.
- **If you are an owner with no subscription agreement, handing over your
  organisation ends your subscription income from it.** Set up an agreement
  first if you want to keep earning from subscriptions there.
- If an owner cannot hand over the organisation, Revelations support can
  appoint a new owner.

### 8. Prices

Organisations and creators set their prices in Studio. Every price is at least
**£1.00**; we do not allow free (£0) offers. When a subscription price changes, current subscribers are emailed,
and the new price applies from their first renewal at least 30 days later.

### Questions for the lawyer (and the owner where marked)

- **P1 — Stripe's Connected Account Agreement.** Stripe requires platforms to
  tell connected accounts that they are bound by its Connected Account Agreement
  and Services Agreement. Exact wording, and where it sits in onboarding?
- **P2 — merchant of record.** We sell to buyers ourselves and pay creators a
  share. Is the creator our supplier, our agent, or neither? This changes the
  wording of §1.
- **P3 — tax.** VAT on digital services sold to UK and overseas buyers; whether
  creators' shares are VAT-inclusive; whether we must report creator earnings to
  HMRC under the digital platform reporting rules (the UK's implementation of the
  OECD model rules).
- **P4 — fee changes.** Owner decided: increases on 30 days' emailed notice,
  decreases at once ("Rises 30 days, cuts now (Recommended)"). Is 30 days enough,
  and must a creator be able to leave without penalty before an increase applies?
- **P5 — unclaimed money.** Owner decided: kept indefinitely, never forfeited,
  with a monthly reminder ("Hold and remind (Recommended)"). Does holding
  creators' money with no end date create any obligation (for example around
  safeguarding or reporting)?
- **P6 — debts.** Owner decided: up to £50 in total is written off after 12 months
  with no earnings; more than £50 is invoiced in full after 90 days unrecovered
  ("Write off after 12 months (Recommended)", amended by the owner — "perhaps
  there should be a limit on the level of absorption we would be willing to take
  before we invoice the creator" — to a "£50 (Recommended)" cap and "All of it,
  after 90 days (Recommended)"). Can we recover a clawback by invoice, and what
  must the terms say for that to be enforceable?
- **P7 — ending agreements at any time.** Is an at-will termination fair to the
  creator, given an agreement is the only way to earn from an organisation's
  subscriptions? Should either side give notice?
